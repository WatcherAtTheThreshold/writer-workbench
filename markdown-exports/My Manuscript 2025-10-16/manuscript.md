# SS 10-9-25 Draft 4

Looking down, John walked forward, he noticed all the small things. His eyes searched for something novel. A little piece of inspiration, maybe a smooth stone, maybe a reflective rock with shining flecks of mica. He noticed micro fly traps and plants with leaves that reminded him of the fractal patterns of nature.  

John crouched down and ran his hands over the ground. He searched wanting to find something secret or lost or hidden. He found a few stones that were worth putting in his pocket.  

Finding a particularly pretty stone, a small purple gem, he pulled out a little red and white striped pouch from under his shirt and slid the gemstone into it. He collected things. Small interesting natural curiosities. One time he found a small, desiccated baby turtle, another time he found a small vole skull that he covered with hot pine pitch that dried into an amber patina. All into the pouch.  

But pouch-worthy or not he searched and collected. By the river was always a good place to find stones and other things. He took his shoes off and followed the sandy bank downriver. He saw something round poking out of the sand. There on the ground was a tarnished ring. He looked around as if someone might be watching though he was miles from any trails or towns. He picked it up, it was old, all metal with a dark patina and no stone. A raised floral pattern wrapped the band. He put it in his pouch.  

A bit further down river, his eyes down, the sand bank ended abruptly. The riverside was a thick tangle of plants blocking the way. An upturned tree crossed the river to a sandbar. From there it connected to the sandy shore on the other side. He paused and scanned around looking for an alternate route. It looked like he could cross the river via downed tree trunk, or he could turn back. Unsteadily he stepped a foot onto the log, with unsure balance he brought the other foot up beside it.  

Both feet down, inch by inch, he shuffled forward. At first it didn't seem so bad, but that feeling only lasted until he couldn't just jump back to shore.  

He wasn't steady, always a little off balance. He nearly pitched over a few times but saved it. heart pounding and dazed with fear, he moved forward.  

The river below was getting deeper. John didn't swim much, but he knew, at least around here, deep river water meant a strong current. Even if only chest or waist high the current would be too strong to cross or swim against. And though the air was warm the crisp mountain water was nearly freezing.  

He thought about turning back but the idea of shimmying his feet in a small circle to turn around was terrifying. He resolved to keep moving, one step after another.  

Then he was falling. He twisted his body and waved his arms to regain equilibrium. He pitched left and right. For a moment he froze in time and was staring straight at the water. A dark abyss invited him, whispering to give up, but he knew the struggle it would entail. Fully clothed in freezing water miles out in the wilderness. Something in him knew that this was an 'end of the line' moment.  

John wasn't sure what happened. He blacked out. One moment about to meet the chilled black river, the next he was perched on all fours holding tight to the downed trunk. He gripped the thick bark tightly, adrenaline shaking his tense limbs. On all fours he continued across.  

Heart still pounding, John smiled as he climbed down onto solid ground. He closed his eyes and took a deep breath, centering himself. "What just happened?" he yelled, "What was that?". Putting a hand to his mouth he looked around in silence.  

The day cooled as the sun fell below the treetops. John pulled a sweatshirt from his backpack and while putting it on he thought about the icy water and the darkness beneath. His wife, his cat, his home, his bed. He missed them. John traced the river back to his car.  

                                                                                     							***   
 
When John pulled into his driveway, the orange glow from the windows against the blue of the house looked beautiful. He walked through the door, the table was set, he was just in time for dinner. Mary, his wife, smiled and waved from the kitchen. Tabitha, their cat, greeted John, weaving between his legs. He dropped his backpack on the floor and walked into the kitchen to see Mary. Putting his arm around her he gave her a kiss on the cheek.  

                                                                                     							***   
 
After dinner, John did the dishes while Mary put the leftovers away. John was talking about making pumpkin pie when Tabitha rushed into the room and let out a loud yowl, and again a second time John and Mary stopped what they were doing and turned to look at her.  

"Are you alright?" asked Mary, in a concerned, high-pitched voice. Mary reached down to pet her and paused; her sides felt stiff. John petted her and agreed, nodding at Mary. Tabitha slunk to her cat bed moving slow. John rubbed a consoling hand on Mary's back. Tabitha's age was a mystery. She had just shown up one rainy night, meowing at the door, thirteen or so years ago.  

"I'll call the vet tomorrow morning", John said to Mary. While the two of them held each other.  

That night John woke up to use the bathroom. Tabitha crossed his mind, I should check on her, he thought. He quietly stepped into the kitchen and looked at her. Still just peacefully sleeping, he felt relieved and returned to bed.  

The next morning John woke up to Mary standing over him. She was softly rubbing her hand on his back. He rolled over, "Good morning", he said, and smiled. "What's going on?", he questioned, his eyes widening and his smile turning to concern.  

Mary was crying, "It's Tabitha, I'm sorry John, she died last night".  

John and Mary held each other. Tabitha was old, this wasn't completely unexpected. But Tabitha was a dear friend who was deeply loved, John and Mary both felt broken hearted.  

Mary found an old hat box and got the blanket that Tabitha slept on. They laid Tabitha in, curled up, and put the lid on the box. John carried the box outside and got a shovel. In a far corner of their yard, by a particularly beautiful apple tree John dug a small square grave. Mary came out of the house as he was finishing up. She carried two cups of coffee, sipping from one and handing the other to him.  

John took the top off of the box, they both looked down on Tabitha's curled up body, she seemed so peaceful. John pulled the pouch from around his neck. He thought something from the pouch would make a fitting funeral gift. He wanted to leave the amber skull, Tabitha loved hunting rodents. Then he thought of the dried baby turtle or the stones. He thought of all the tiny, beautiful moments, the pieces that make up a life, so simple, yet so mysterious. Holding the pouch John hesitated for a moment. Tabitha was laying there as if she was just asleep. He stroked her fur one last time. With tears rolling down his cheeks he tucked the pouch gently against her. Mary picked some catnip from the garden and laid it around her body like a wreath of garland, last, Mary added a single, white, daisy.  

John put the lid on the box and gently laid it in the bottom of the grave; it was a perfect fit. He filled the hole and smoothed it over. Holding hands, they both said their goodbyes to Tabitha.  

                                                                                     							***   
 
The next morning John and Mary walked to Tabitha's grave under the apple tree. Each sipped a warm cup of coffee. The mood was hushed and low. Like a veil of sadness, Tabitha's death still hung over the house. In whispering tones, they chatted about the day ahead.  

While crossing the yard, John froze, Tabitha's grave looked like something had dug it up?  

"I should have dug it deeper and put some rocks on top", John said, frustration in his voice. He jogged forward to investigate, coffee sloshing out of his mug. He feared seeing some gory scene, but Tabitha was gone, just gone. A part of him felt relieved but her missing body was no real consolation. John had a sick feeling in his stomach.  

Mary walked up beside him, "Something dug her up?", her mouth open, eyes flush with anger.  

"A dog, I guess, or a coyote", said John, shrugging his shoulders. He hated the thought of something carrying Tabitha away. He found the shovel to fill in the hole. About to start he noticed his pouch still laying in the grave. Spearing his shovel into the ground, he reached into the grave and retrieved the red and white striped pouch. He tossed it on the ground and solemnly filled the hole.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
On his way into the house, John set the pouch on a porch table. Mary gave John a tight hug before heading off to work for the day.  

"I love you", he said as she stepped off of the porch.  

"I love you too", she said, "I'll see you tonight". John watched her walk away down the sidewalk. He opened the front door and stopped, remembering that Tabitha wouldn't be there.  

He gathered his things, found his keys and headed out the door, locking it before leaving. He backed their little hatchback into the road and drove off to work.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
That night after dinner, Mary and John sat at the kitchen table and talked. John had picked up the pouch and was now feeling the items through the thin muslin fabric.  

Mary noticed him fiddling with it, she asked, "What is in your pouch?".  

John pulled at the drawstring, loosening the top. Forming a half circle with one hand to block anything from rolling away, he dumped the pouch's contents onto the table.  

Mary picked up the amber skull and examined it closely. Setting the miniature skull back down she poked at the tiny turtle, pushing it an inch or two across the table. She looked up and gave John a questioning look; he just shrugged. She moved some of the small stones around with her finger to get a look at them. She picked up the ring and tried it on. She pursed her lips and made a face that said, 'what do you think'? John nodded with approval.  

Mary tried to remove the ring, but it was stuck. "Oh no", she said "It won't come off".  

John smiled and said, "are you pretending because you like it or is it actually stuck on your finger?"  

"I can't get it off, but I do like it. Maybe I'll wear it for a while". They both smiled and laughed. John slid the skull, then the turtle and all the little stones, one by one, back into the pouch.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
The next morning John went downstairs before Mary. He made coffee and got a cup. Walking to the porch John yelled with a start, spilling coffee everywhere. Mary ran down the stairs. "What the hell!" he was yelling as she walked up to him. "I swear, Tabitha was just sitting, right here, on the porch. She ran when I yelled but it looked exactly like her. I was sure it was her". He doubted that it was actually her but what are the chances of something so uncanny. A cat that looks exactly like Tabitha showing up on the porch just days after she died.  

"Strange", Mary said to him, before going in to get coffee. She reached out offering to take his empty mug, he handed it to her and after looking at the grave for another minute followed her inside.  

"I think we're still a bit shaken", she said, unsure of what to make of John's encounter. They did their normal morning routines and headed their separate ways for the day. Mary walked down the sidewalk as John drove away.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
Mary glanced back and watched as their car turned the corner. Things had been stressful since Tabitha's death, she thought to herself. Did John see Tabitha's ghost this morning? Was that some kind of stress induced episode? The idea was a little creepy, but she didn't hate the thought of spirit animals that follow us in their afterlife, she smiled at the image of a ghost Tabitha tagging along beside her.  

She passed a house with large, old lilac bushes in the yard, she slowed as she walked through the fragrance, inhaling deeply.  

Noticing the ring on her finger she gave it a few more tugs. This morning, before going downstairs for coffee she had oiled her finger, hoping the ring would just slide off; It didn't. That was when John yelled about the ghost cat, she smiled to herself again, shaking her head. She would rather have a ghost cat than crazy husband. She giggled out loud at this. Maybe I'm the crazy one, she thought.  

She stopped into a coffee shop for a coffee and cruller. She was the only customer at the counter, so she chatted with the owner. Mary enjoyed these neighborhood interactions.  

With her coffee in one hand and half eaten cruller in the other she said thanks and with a warm smile waved goodbye. Walking out the door she continued on her way.  

She put in her earbuds to listen to some music. The Carpenters, 'We’ve Only Just Begun' started playing.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
When John arrived at work, he heard sirens in the distance but thought nothing of it. Later, during his morning break, John messaged Mary. The fifteen-minute break passed, and John didn't hear back from her. Must be busy, he thought as he returned to the office. But then the phone on his desk rang, he answered expecting it to be work related. It was the police. They asked him his name and if his wife was Mary. The officer on the other end of the phone apologized and said " There's been an accident. Your wife didn't make it...".  

John didn't hear the rest, he couldn't bear to even try. Mary was in an accident, some kind of hit and run. She was at the hospital, and they wanted him to come look at the body. It didn't feel real, it wasn't real. A pain in John's chest was opening up, spreading out of his body and engulfing his entire reality. He set the phones receiver on the desk, he could still hear the officer talking as he walked away, a glassy eyed, confused look on his face.  

He got into his car and started it, but he didn't know what hospital he was supposed to go to. Hopeless and lost he turned the car off. He held his hands to his face and cried. 

But wait, he thought, this must all be wrong. Wiping the tears from his cheeks he decided to drive to the police station to clear everything up. To pick up Mary, he thought. A spark of hope lit across his face.  

He started the car and drove straight to the police station. Walking in he was met with serious, somber faces. "Can I help you" said an officer sitting behind a desk. "Yes Please", he said "I'm looking for my wife Mary, I got a phone call".  

The officer's shoulders slumped, "John, right?" He said, standing up, he put his hand on John's shoulder. John just shook his head yes. "Is there someone we can call for you? Is there someone who could pick you up?" John shook his head no with tears starting again. "I'm very sorry John, why don't you sit here while we figure out what we're going to do".  

John sat, lost in a numb fog, scared to think of anything, scared of the pain that might be uncovered.  

The officer explained to John what they think happened. John, again, barely heard what was being said. Someone driving a large white pick-up ran a red light, hopped a curb and struck Mary. He assured John that it happened really quick, Mary wasn't in any pain, her passing was instant. They didn't find a purse. John Sobbed. Again, the officer asked if there was anyone, they could call but this time it was to go identify the body. John assured him that he was alright to go and he thanked the officer. "I'm sorry John", said the officer while John walked away. He cried again when he got in the car. He curled his fist and punched the steering wheel. After composing himself he started the car and drove off to the hospital where Mary's corpse waited.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
He drove with his mouth open, eyes empty, he was a husk. How would he react to seeing Mary, lying there, lifeless. He was absolutely terrified.  

He curved up the winding drive to the hospital. Swirling clouds darkened the sky, it started raining. I'm just not going to think about anything until I talk to someone who knows what's going on, John resolved. He stepped from the car; the rain was light. One drop landed on his hand; he watched it spread and dissipate. Another drop hit his lip, John tasted it.  

He walked through two large glass sliding doors. An old man sat behind a reception desk, John approached.  

"Hello, what can I help you with?" asked the old man, his face wrinkled and eyes smiling. John looked around, the crisp clean corners, the plastic look of the plants. It all looked fake and sterile, like some backrooms fever-dream. "Do you have an appointment, or can I direct you somewhere?" continued the man behind the desk.  

John looked around, internally bristling with anxiety and fear, "Yes I'm here to see my wife, well, my wife's body", John's face grimaced in pain. He looked down noticing the different colors in the loop weave of the reception rooms carpeted floor.  

"I see", said the man behind the desk, folding his hands nervously. "What's your wife's name", he asked, eyes narrow with concern.  

John replied with Mary's full name and after finding information in the computer the old man sent him to the elevator, the morgue was in the basement. He pressed the button marked 'B', the elevator moved. The elevator stopped, doors sliding open. The unpleasant smell of antiseptic washed over him. He approached another reception desk.  

"Can I help you?" asked the woman sitting behind the desk. She typed the information into the computer and said, "I'll be right back in a moment, could you wait here". and she walked into the back of the morgue.  

John stood there under the tinged green glow of the fluorescent lights. He reached out and touched a countertop, cold, stainless steel. He ran his hand, palm down, over the smooth surface.  

The woman walked back into the room. "John, there's been some kind of mix-up...", her voice cracking, she paused and coughed to clear her throat.  

John felt cold and numb. It's happening again, he thought, it's getting worse, it keeps getting worse.  

"Your wife...", she cleared her throat again, "your wife's body should be here. The computer says she's here, but we can't find her." She looked around as if searching for something.  

John was confused, "Is she not dead?" It came out slow, his large, baggy, red eyes looked scared to hope.  

The woman looked down, then raised her gaze to look John in the eyes, "I'm sorry John, she is deceased, and her body should be here. I have no idea what could have happened. We'll have to check other hospitals. Do you need someone to call? I have to make some phone calls about this. Do the police know how to reach you once this is sorted?"  

John shook his head yes, looking away from her, confused and hurting. He turned to leave and felt a hand on his back. "I'm sorry", said the woman, trying to be comforting. John walked away, head lowered, barely noticing the gesture.  

It was raining harder now; John switched on the windshield wipers. He thought about not going home, no one was there. Mary wouldn't be, Tabitha wouldn't be. He was alone with this incredible weight in his chest, this deep ache. But there wasn't anywhere else to go.  

He pulled into the driveway. The clouded, rainy, weather made the house look grey and diffuse. With no lights on the windows were black like the empty, cold, river water. The hollow feeling house brought that same chilly dread.  

                                                                                     							***  
 
John tried to make dinner alone that night. Cooking rice and beans on the stove top, he set down a plate and got a wooden serving spoon. Pausing, he picked up the plate with both hands and brought it smashing down on the tiled counter. It shattered into hundreds of pieces. Holding the shards that remained in his hands, he loosened his shoulders and cried. The shards fell to the floor. He looked around at all the tiny slivers of ceramic.  

John got the broom and swept all into a pile. He noticed a small drop of blood on his hand. He noticed the diamond shape pattern of the broken ceramic shards as they slid off of the dustpan into the trash bin.  

Getting a fresh plate, he ate, then washed dishes. Slowly, he shuffled off to bed, climbing the stairs towards his empty bedroom.  

He prepared for bed and lay down, hoping for sleep, fearing thoughts and memories. He buried his face in a pillow and quickly drifted off to sleep.  

                                                                                     							***  
 

John awoke with the feeling that he was being watched. "Mary?", he said aloud, as if talking to a ghost or dream. Something made a noise in the darkness from across the room. John went still, he scanned around, waiting for his eyes to adjust to the darkness. John was terrified, he assumed someone had broken into the house and was now in the room with him, staring at him from the darkness. A scared, "Hello", was all he could get out.  

Something, a small animal, ran from the room and thumped down the stairs.  

John leapt from the bed. Putting his pants on he felt a cold breeze. Barefoot and shirtless he ran down the stairs. The front door was wide open. The curtains on the door billowed and waved out into the night. He pushed the door to close it; the wind gusted and slammed it shut. He hoped the animal from upstairs had run out the door. He walked into the kitchen to check the back door before going back to bed.  

There in the dark kitchen was Mary, her back was turned, she stood over the sink staring into the backyard. Layers of tension melted from Johns shoulders; he was engulfed with a warm relief. The broken heart that had spread into infinite, tiny fragments, out across the universe, it re-solidified. He was whole again. His eyes grew wide and starry; his strained expression softened into a smile. "I knew it", he exhaled.  

He stood staring at her; she didn't say a word or move at all. John couldn't move. limbs heavy, he was frozen. Had time stopped? Am I dreaming? Nothing moved, nothing made a sound.  

Then, Tabitha ran past the kitchen door, this time he was positive it was her.  

Mary turned away from the window and looked down at the floor. The slow movement, the oddly sloped shoulders... Something was off... Johns smile faltered as a single tear rolled down to his chin and fell to the floor.

# 10-14- Draft 5

Travis drove through the cold desert; the sun just beginning to peak over the purple mountaintops in the distance. He was going to Levi's and Carmen's houses, though he hadn't been there for ten years. He didn't know if they'd be home, but Carmen's mother Rosa should be. So, he headed to Carmen's house first.  

Pulling off the main road, he drove onto the dusty dirt path that led to Carmen's house. His truck bumped and jostled over rocks and potholes. Cresting a rise, a coyote stood in his way, glowing yellow eyes reflecting his headlights. It looked lanky, shoulders raised, head hanging low. It stared at him, panting, head bobbing rhythmically. It's breath visible in the cool morning air.  

Travis turned the radio down, the coyote perked its ears, lifting its head, it ran into the desert. Travis glanced at the box, laying in the passenger seat before lifting his foot off the brake. The truck slowly tugged forward.  

He rolled up to the house and shut off his engine. Stepping out, he stretched and yawned. Small desert birds flew about singing their morning songs.  

He got back into the truck, laying his seat as far back as it would go, and fell asleep.  

He awoke to stifling the heat. The bright desert sun blinded him. Blinking and tired Travis put on sunglasses and stepped from the truck; the air smelled like a campfire. He got the box from his passenger seat and walked towards the front door. He knocked but no one answered; a dog lazily barked from inside. He pictured an old dog willing to bark but not willing to stand up to do so; he chuckled.  

He set the box by the door and turned left. But paused, changed his mind, and picked the box back up. He put it back in the passenger seat and shut the door. From the desert, someone called his name. He looked around. Carmen's mother, Rosa, came into view.  

"Travis, how are you?" She asked, as she walked towards him, she was slightly lifting the front of her dress so it wouldn't drag on the ground.  

"Hello Rosa, how are you, you look beautiful as ever", They smiled and hugged. Her perfume was soft, almost muted, but it stayed with him after she pulled away. Travis couldn't place the pleasant scent, but he liked it.  

"Thank you", she blushed and smiled at him, a shine in her eyes. "Carmen isn't home. She's in the mountains with Levi; they've been gone for days." She lifted her chin, peering off into the desert. "They should be home anytime, but maybe not", she laughed. "Come inside", she invited him. "I'm making lunch". Not waiting for a response, she looped her arm through Travis's and walked him into the house.  

Two pots sat cooking on the big iron woodstove, Rosa stirred each one, steam rising into the air. As Travis had guessed, she had an old, white-faced dog. A retriever lay on the floor in the corner. It wanted to bark again, lifting its head and preparing to stand, but it was still lazy. It closed its eyes it went back to sleep.  

"This is Arlo", said Rosa, noticing Travis looking at the dog.  

"I heard him bark when I knocked on the door, I assumed he'd be an old guy", Travis smiled.  

Rosa nodded, smiling and blinking, "yup, I don't let him wander far these days, too many coyotes about".  

"I saw one not far down the road", Travis could smell the refried beans, and the tortillas Rosa was cooking in a flat pan. His stomach rumbled.  

"Oh, I brought some gifts for everyone, I'll go get them." and he walked out the door.  

Travis was halfway to the truck when a coyote walked out from behind it. It turned its head to look at Travis. He maintained eye contact, unsure what else to do. He stopped but then slowly continued forward assuming the coyote would move on, but it didn't, Travis stopped again. "Hmm..." Travis said aloud. "What are we doing?". The coyote looked unbothered but continued to stare, tongue out slightly, panting in the heat.  

Travis started to turn back towards the house but as soon as he moved the coyote dug its paws into the dry, hard ground, as if to run. Travis couldn't tell what that could mean, so he stopped moving.  

The coyote started to walk away from him but diagonally, and its semi-circled back getting between Travis and the house. Closer to the truck inched up to it and hopped in. Travis honked his horn, and the coyote ran off behind the house.  

Rosa stepped out of the door, "What are you out here honking about?" She yelled at Travis, who was sitting in his passenger seat, awkwardly squashed beside the box. "Are you out here scared and honking?" They both laughed. Travis pictured himself as a frightened goose, honking in the yard.  

He got out of the truck and looked around nervously, picking up the box before shutting the door. "That coyote was here", he began to explain.  

"That coyote, the same one you saw earlier?" Rosa asked with a smile, clearly teasing him.  

"Not the same one, well maybe, I don't know. It seemed to want to block my way," Travis said, heart still beating hard.  

"So, a coyote was out here playing football with you or something", She said laughing at him.  

Travis was confused; he didn't know what the coyote was doing. The old dog slowly walked out of the house and barked at him, as if he'd just arrived, then turned, dismissively and walked back indoors. Rosa watched Arlo and laughed.  

"Burritos are ready," she said, raising her eyebrows and motioning towards the house. Travis's stomach rumbled again. They walked inside; Arlo lifted his head from his pillow in the corner.  

                                                                                                    ***     
 

They walked in and Travis set the box on the floor. He pulled out a bottle of wine from it which he handed to Rosa; she read the label as she brought it to the kitchen. He pulled out a six pack of glass bottled sodas and walked it to the fridge.  

"Would you like one of these?" He asked Rosa, she nodded, he pulled two out and closed the fridge. Using a wall mounted bottle opener, he opened them and handed her one. She took a swig and picked up her plate, Travis got his, and they both walked into the living room to sit and eat.  

"So, what happened with your coyote outside?" Rosa asked, smirking.  

"I don't know, it seemed like it was confronting me?" Travis ate another bite and drank a sip of soda. "I don't know how to describe it. It stared at me. then walked around me until I honked at it.  

"Maybe it doesn't like your car?" Rosa said, Travis couldn't tell if she was just picking on him again. "Maybe it was a Bruja transformed" she said, cocking her head knowingly, raising her hands in a 'who knows' gesture.  

"Why would a witch in the shape of a coyote confront me in your dooryard?" Travis joked, poking back at her, teasing him.  

"Maybe 'she' doesn't like your car?" Said Rosa playfully. Travis smiled but thought it was creepy, all the witch talk.  

He didn't believe in brujas, but he didn't like the idea of one stalking out by his truck, nonetheless.  

Arlo walked up to Travis, slow and arthritic, to beg for his last bite. Travis looked at Rosa, motioning towards Arlo with his last bite. Rosa shook her head, yes. Travis reached out his hand, and the old dog carefully and gently took the last of burrito from his hand and returned to his corner pillow, swallowing the bite before laying back down.  

"It's strange that it was confronting you," said Rosa. This further unsettled Travis but when he looked up at her face, she was wearing a huge grin. "You're not going to get scared and go back out and honk more are you?" She asked, laughing harder this time; she slapped her leg, clearly amused by her own joking.  

                                                                                                    ***
