# #2 10-10-25 Draft 4

John sat at a tall table in the window, just observing people, passersby on the sidewalk, the barista behind the counter, customers in line. 

The barista floated between the cash register and the coffee machines, taking and filling orders. John sat watching the interaction as customers came in and left with coffees and pastries in hand.  An older woman stepped up to order, she was very polite. Leaving a tip as she walked away, the barista smiled with genuine warmth. The next man in line was tall and carried an air of displeasure. The barista withdrew by not making eye contact, he shrank in his posture. The man seemed to contemplate leaving a tip, then pocketed his change and walked out.

For some reason John could see through people. Not with X-ray vision, but with an intuitive hunch about the many branching paths that make up a human life. He saw why they did the things they did, acted the ways they acted, felt the ways they felt. With one look, one interaction, he wouldn't intuit exactly what had happened to them. But his mind would reverse engineer the probable paths that formed them into who they were.

John turned on his stool and returned his gaze to people walking by on the sidewalk. His laptop was open, cursor blinking on a blank page. He wrote a line and erased it. Closing the laptop, he drank the last sip of his coffee, stood and pushed his stool in. Sliding the laptop into his messenger bag he fastened the soft leather clasps before walking out the door.  

John half tripped off the concrete stoop outside of the coffee shop. He bumped straight into a woman walking by. He jostled backwards, she fell forwards, he grabbed  her arms to steady them both. 

"I'm sorry" John blurted out. "are you alright?"

"I'm OK" she said, flustered "I'm sorry, I wasn't watching where I was going".

They both smiled and looked around, gathering themselves. When they looked up at each other John was struck by a sense of déjà vu, something in her smile and her eyes. John quickly realized that he couldn't read her. She didn't seem to be acting out some role. Maybe she was... just her. She noticed flour  specked on John's shirt.

"Baking?" she asked, her eyebrows raised.

"Pumpkin pie", he replied, with a smile. "It's on the counter at home, cooling right now." maybe because he couldn't tell what she was thinking John took a chance he wouldn't normally take. "Care to try some?" he asked, putting a hand out. 

She took his hand "sure," she said. "Well, how far away do you live?"

Fearing she was changing her mind, John said "Two blocks down the street",  he pointed towards his apartment.

"Perfect, let's eat pie", they walked back to Johns apartment.

                                                                                                                ***

With a fork-full of pie and a sip of coffee in his mouth John asked "Do you live here in the city?".

"I do", she said, swallowing pie and then sipping coffee to wash it down. "I just moved here three weeks ago", She sipped her coffee. "I like it, I'm getting used to it, haven't found a job yet", she said, shrugging her shoulders.

The job subject seemed touchy so John shifted direction, "Where are you from, I'm guessing the East coast?" He was just glad she agreed to a pie date.

"Yup, way East coast, Northern New Hampshire, nearly Canada. And good pie by the way, this is really, really good", she took another bite.

"No way!" John was shocked. "I'm from New Hampshire too,  I grew up in Lancaster. Where are you from Pittsburg, Colebrook?"

"What, that's crazy, I grew up in Colebrook" she replied.

"I say I'm from Northern New Hampshire, but you are truly from Northern New Hampshire", he chuckled and shook his head marveling at the coincidence.

After pie, before Mary left, they made plans for another date. Mary felt bubbly and energized. John was beaming.
