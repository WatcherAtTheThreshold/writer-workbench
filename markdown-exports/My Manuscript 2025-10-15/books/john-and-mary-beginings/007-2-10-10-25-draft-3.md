# #2 10-10-25 Draft 3

Carmen sat at a tall table in the window, just observing people, passersby on the sidewalk, the barista behind the counter, customers in line. 

The barista floated between the cash register and the coffee machines, taking and filling orders. Carmen sat watching the interaction as customers came in and left with coffees and pastries in hand.  An older woman stepped up to order, she was very polite. Leaving a tip as she walked away, the barista smiled with genuine warmth. The next man in line was tall and carried an air of displeasure. The barista withdrew by not making eye contact, he shrank in his posture. the man seemed to contemplate a tip, then pocketed his change and walked out.

For some reason Carmen could see through people. Not with X-ray vision, but with an intuitive hunch about the many branching paths that make up a human life. He saw why they did the things they did, acted the ways they acted, felt the ways they felt. With one look, one interaction, he wouldn't intuit exactly what had happened to them. But his mind would reverse engineer the probable paths that formed them into who they were.

Carmen turned on his stool and returned his gaze to people walking by on the sidewalk. His laptop was open, cursor blinking on a blank page. He wrote a line and erased it. Closing the laptop, he drank the last sip of his coffee, stood and pushed his stool in. Sliding the laptop into his messenger bag he fastened the soft leather clasps before walking out the door.  

Carmen half tripped off the concrete stoop outside of the coffee shop. He bumped straight into a woman walking by. He jostled backwards, she fell forwards, he grabbed  her arms to steady them both. 

"I'm sorry" Carmen blurted out. "are you alright?"

"I'm OK" she said, flustered "I'm sorry, I wasn't watching where I was going".

They both smiled and looked around, gathering themselves. When they looked up at each other Carmen was struck by a sense of déjà vu, something in her smile and her eyes. Carmen quickly realized that he couldn't read her. She didn't seem to be acting out some role. Maybe she was... just her. She noticed flour  specked on Carmen's shirt.

"Baking?" she asked, her eyebrows raised.

"Cookies", he replied, smiling.
