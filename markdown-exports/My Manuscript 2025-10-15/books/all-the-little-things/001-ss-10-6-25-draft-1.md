# SS 10-6-25 Draft 1

John slowly moved forward. Looking down, he noticed all the small things. His eyes searched for something novel. A little piece of inspiration, maybe a smooth stone, maybe a reflective rock with shining flecks of mica. He noticed plants, small micro fly traps and plants with leaves that reminded him of the fractal patterns of nature.  

John crouched down and ran his hands, flat, palms down over the ground. He searched like he might find something secret or lost or hidden. He found a few stones that were worth putting in his pocket to examine later.  

Finding a particularly pretty stone, it looked like a small purple gem, he pulled out a little red and white striped pouch from under his shirt and slid the gemstone into it. He collected things. Small interesting natural bits. One time he found a small, desiccated baby turtle, another time he found a small vole skull that he covered with hot pine pitch that dried into a solid sepia patina. All into the pouch.  

But pouch-worthy or not he searched and collected. By the river was always a good place to find stones and other things. He took off his shoes and followed the sandy banks downriver. Something on the ground reflected blinding light into his eyes. He shielded his eyes and looked closer. There on the ground was a tarnished ring. He looked around as if someone might be watching though he was miles from any trails or towns. He picked it up to examine it, it looked old, all metal with a dark patina and no stone. A raised floral pattern wrapped around the band. He put it into his pouch.  

A bit further down the river, head down, the sand bank ended abruptly. The riverside was a thick tangle of plants blocking passage. An upturned tree crossed the river to a sandbar that connected to the sandy shore on the other side. He paused and scanned around looking for an alternate route. It looked like he could cross the river via downed tree or turn back. Unsteadily he stepped a foot onto the log, with unsure balance he brought the other foot up to meet it. 

With both feet on the log, he shuffled forward, inch by inch. At first it didn't seem so bad, but that feeling only lasted until he was out of 'jumping back to shore' range.  

He nearly pitched over but regained his balance,  heart pounding and dazed, he moved forward.  

He could see the river below getting deeper. John didn't do much swimming, but he knew, at least around here, deep river water meant a strong current. Even if only waist or chest deep the current would be too strong to cross or swim against. And though the air was warm the crisp mountain water was nearly still freezing.  

He thought about turning back but the idea of shuffling his feet in a small circle to face the other direction was terrifying. He resolved to keep moving forward.  One small step after another.

Out of nowhere he was falling again. He twisted his body and waved his arms to regain equilibrium. He pitched left and right. He bent back then forward. For a moment he froze in time and was staring straight at the water. A dark abyss invited him, but he quietly knew the struggle it would ask. Fully clothed in freezing water miles out in the wilderness. Something in him deeply felt that this was potentially an 'end of the line' moment.  

John wasn't sure what happened. He blacked out. One moment about to meet the chilled black river, the next perched on all fours holding tight to the log. He was in shock. He gripped the log tightly, adrenaline shaking his tense limbs. He continued his scared shuffle the rest of the way across the log but stayed on all fours for the remainder of the crossing.  

Heart still pounding, John smiled as he climbed down onto solid ground. He closed his eyes and took a deep breath, centering himself. "What just happened?" he yelled, "What was that?". Putting a hand to his mouth he looked around in silence. 

The day cooled as the sun fell below the treetops. John pulled a sweatshirt from his backpack and while putting it on he thought about the icy water and the darkness beneath. His wife, his cat, his home, his bed. He missed them. John traced the river back to his car.  

                                                                                                 *** 
 
John pulled into his driveway, the orange glow from the windows against the blue of the house looked beautiful. He walked inside, the table was set, he was just in time for dinner. Mary, his wife, smiled and waved from the kitchen. Tabitha, the cat, weaved in between his legs, excited to see him. He dropped his backpack on the floor and walked into the kitchen. Putting his arm around Mary, John gave her a gentle kiss on the cheek. 

                                                                                                 *** 
 
After dinner, John did the dishes while Mary put the leftovers away. John was talking about making pumpkin pie when Tabitha rushed into the room and let out a loud yowl, she yowled a second time, both John and Mary stopped what they were doing.  

"Are you alright?" asked Mary, in a concerned, high-pitched voice. Mary reached down to pet Tabitha and paused; her sides felt stiff. John touched her and agreed, nodding at Mary. They watched Tabitha slink over to her cat bed and lay down. John rubbed a consoling hand on Mary's back.  

Tabitha's age was a mystery. She had just shown up one rainy night, meowing at the door, thirteen or so years ago. "I'll call the vet tomorrow morning", he said to Mary. The two held each other a little tighter. 

That night John woke up to use the bathroom. Tabitha crossed his mind, I should check on her, he thought. He quietly stepped into the kitchen and looked at her. Still just peacefully sleeping, he felt relieved and returned to bed. 

The next morning John woke up to Mary standing over him, softly rubbing her hand on his back. He rolled over, "Good morning", he said, and smiled. "What's going on?", he questioned, his eyes widening and his smile turning to concern.  

Mary was crying, "It's Tabitha, she died last night".  

John and Mary held each other, and they both cried. Tabitha was old, this wasn't completely unexpected. But Tabitha was a dear friend who was deeply loved, John and Mary both felt broken hearted. 

Mary found an old hat box and lined it with a blanket that Tabitha often slept on. They put Tabitha in and put the top on the box. John carried the box outside and got a shovel. In a far corner of their yard, by a particularly beautiful apple tree John dug a small square grave. Mary came out of the house as he was finishing digging. She carried two cups of coffee, sipping from one and handing the other to John. 

John took the top off of the box and they both looked at Tabitha's curled up body, she looked so peaceful. John pulled the pouch from around his neck. He wanted to leave the vole skull, Tabitha loved hunting rodents. Then he thought of leaving the dried baby turtle. And the stones. He thought of all the tiny, beautiful moments, the pieces that make up a life, simple yet mysterious. John hesitated for a moment, holding the pouch he looked down on Tabitha laying there as if she was just asleep. He stroked her fur one last time. With tears rolling down his cheeks he tucked the pouch gently against her soft fur. Mary picked some catnip from the garden and laid it around her body like a wreath of garland, last, Mary added a single, white, daisy. 

John put the lid on the box and put it in the bottom of the grave; it was a nearly perfect fit. He filled the hole and smoothed the dirt flat. Holding hands, they both said their goodbyes to their beloved Tabitha.   

                                                                                                 *** 
 
 The next morning, John and Mary, walked to the grave under the apple tree. Each sipped a warm cup of coffee The mood was hushed and low. Like a veil of sadness, Tabitha's death still hung over them. Just whispering they chatted about the day ahead. 

As they walked across the yard, John froze, Tabitha's grave looked like something had dug it up? "I should have dug deeper or put some rocks on top", he said to Mary apologetically. He jogged forward to investigate, coffee sloshing out of his mug with each step. He feared seeing some gory scene with Tabitha's body but she was just gone. A part of him felt relieved but her being gone was no real consolation. John was left feeling frustrated and sick to his stomach.

Mary walked up beside him, "Something dug her up?" she said, clearly disheartened.   

"A dog, I guess, or a coyote", John added, shrugging his shoulders. He hated the thought of something carrying Tabitha off into the night. He went and found the shovel to fill in the hole. About to start shoveling he noticed his pouch still laying at the bottom.  Spearing the shovel into the ground, he reached into the grave and pulled out the red and white striped pouch. Not wanting to put something in his pocket that had been in a grave, he tossed it on the ground and solemnly filled in the hole.  

                                                                                                 *** 

On his way into the house, John set the pouch on the table on their porch. They both showered and dressed. Mary gave John a tight hug before heading off to work for the day. 

"I love you", he said as she stepped off of the porch.

"I love you too", she said, "I'll see you tonight". John watched her walk away down the sidewalk. Walking back inside he opened the door and stopped, remembering that Tabitha wouldn't be there. 

He gathered his things, found his keys and headed out the door, locking it before leaving. He backed their little hatchback into the road and drove off to work.

                                                                                                 ***

That night after dinner, Mary and John sat at the kitchen table and talked. John had picked the pouch up when he got home and was now feeling the items it contained through the thin muslin fabric. 

Mary noticed him holding it, she asked, "What is in your pouch?".

John pulled at the drawstring, loosening the top. Forming a half circle with one hand to block anything from rolling away, he dumped the pouch's contents onto the table.

Mary picked up the vole skull and examined it closely. Setting the miniature skull back down she poked at the tiny turtle, pushing it an inch or two across the table. She looked up and gave John a questioning look; he just shrugged. She moved some of the small stones around with her finger to get a look at them. She picked up the ring and tried it on. Looking at John she showed off the ring on her finger, pursing her lips she made a face that said 'what do you think'? John nodded with approval.

Mary tried to remove the ring but found it was stuck. "Oh no", she said "It won't come off". 

"Are you pretending because you like it or will it really not come off?" John asked Mary playfully.

"I can't get it off, but I do like it. Maybe I'll wear it for a while". They both smiled as John slid the skull, then the turtle and all the little stones, one by one, back into the pouch. 

                                                                                                 ***

The next morning John went downstairs before Mary. He made coffee and got a cup before heading out onto the porch. John yelled with a start, spilling coffee all over the porch. Mary ran down the stairs. "What the hell!" he said as she got to the door. "I swear, Tabitha was just sitting on the porch. She ran when I yelled but it looked exactly like her. I was sure it was her". He doubted that it was actually her but what are the chances of something so uncanny. A cat that looks exactly like Tabitha showing up on the porch just days after she died.

"Strange", Mary said to him, on her way in to get coffee. She reached out her hand offering to take his now empty mug, he handed it to her and followed her inside. "I think we're still a bit shaken", she said, unsure of what to make of John's encounter. They did their normal morning routines and headed their separate ways for the day. Mary walked down the sidewalk and John drove away.

                                                                                                 ***

Mary heard John drive away, glancing back she watched as he turned the corner. Things had been stressful since Tabitha's death, she thought to herself. Did John see Tabitha's ghost this morning? Was that some kind of stress induced episode? The idea was a little creepy but she didn't hate the thought of spirit animals that follow us as their afterlife, she smiled at the image of a transparent Tabitha tagging along. 

She passed a house with large, old lilac bushes in the yard, she slowed as she walked through the fragrance, inhaling deeply. 

Noticing the ring on her finger she gave it a few tugs, before going downstairs for coffee this morning she had oiled the ring and her finger, hoping it would just slide off. It didn't. That was when John yelled about the ghost cat, she smiled to herself again. She would rather have a ghost cat than crazy husband, this made her giggle out loud. Maybe I'm the crazy one, she thought.

She stopped into the coffee shop for her coffee and cruller, her usual. She was the only customer at the counter so she chatted with Sara, the owner.  Mary enjoyed these neighborhood interactions. 

With her coffee in one hand and half eaten cruller in the other she thanked Sara and with a warm smile she said goodbye. Walking out the door she continued on her way to work.

She took out her earbuds and put on some music. The Carpenters, 'We’ve Only Just Begun' started playing. They Loved this song.

                                                                                                 ***

When John arrived at work he heard sirens in the distance but thought nothing of it. Two hours later, during his morning break, John messaged Mary. The fifteen minute break passed and John didn't hear back from her. Must be busy, he thought as he returned to his office. But then the phone on his desk rang, he answered expecting it to be work related. It was the police. They asked him his name and if his wife was Mary. The officer on the other end of the phone apologized and said " There's been an accident. Your wife didn't make it...".

John didn't hear the rest, he couldn't bear to even try. Mary was in an accident, some kind of hit and run. She was at the hospital  and they wanted him to come look at the body. It didn't feel real, it wasn't real. A pain in Johns chest was opening up, spreading out of his body and engulfing his entire reality. He set the phones receiver on the desk, he could still hear the officer talking as he walked away with  glassy eyes and a confused look on his face. 

He got into his car and started it but he didn't know what hospital he was supposed to go to. Hopeless and lost he turned the car off and cried holding his face in his hands. But wait, he thought, this must all be wrong. Wiping the tears from his face he decided to drive to the police station to clear everything up. To pick up Mary he thought, a spark of hope lit across his face.

He started the car and drove straight to the police station. Walking in he was met with serious, stiff faces. "Can I help you" said an officer sitting behind a desk. "Yes Please", he said "I'm looking for my wife Mary, I got a phone call". 

The officers shoulders slumped, "John, right?" He said, standing up, reaching a hand to Johns shoulder. John just shook his head yes. "Is there someone we can call for you? You seem very distraught, I think you should go home and we can deal with all this later. Is there someone who could pick you up?" John shook his head no with tears starting again. "I'm very sorry John, why don't you sit here while we figure out what  we're going to do".

John sat, lost in a numb fog, scared to think of anything, scared of the pain that it would uncover. 

The officer explained to John what they think happened. John, again, barely heard what was being said. Someone driving a large white pick-up ran a red light, hopped a curb and struck Mary. He assured John that it happened really quick, Mary wasn't in any pain, her passing was instant. They didn't locate a purse or wallet. John Sobbed. Again the officer asked if there was anyone they could call but this time to go identify the body. John assured him that he was alright to go and he thanked the officer out of courtesy and shock. "I'm sorry John", the officer replied as John walked away. John cried again when he got in his car. With a curled fist he struck his steering wheel. He composed himself, started the car and drove off to the hospital  where Mary's corpse waited. 

                                                                                                 ***

He drove with his mouth open, eyes empty, he was a husk. How would he react to seeing Mary, lying there, lifeless. He was absolutely terrified. 

He curved up the winding driveway to the hospital. Swirling clouds darkened the sky and it started to rain. I'm just not going to think about anything until I talk to someone who knows what's going on, John resolved.  He stepped from the car, the rain was light. One drop landed on his hand, he watched it spread and dissipate. Another drop hit his lip, John tasted it. 

He walked through two large glass sliding doors. An old man sat behind a front desk, John approached.

"Hello, what can I help you with today?" asked the old man, his face wrinkled and eyes smiling. John looked around, the crisp clean corners, the plastic look that all the plants had. It looked fake and sterile, like some backrooms fever-dream. "Do you have an appointment or can I direct you somewhere?" continued the man behind the desk.

John looked around, internally bristling with anxiety and fear, "Yes I'm here to see my wife, well, my wife's body", Johns face grimaced in pain. He looked down, dissociating, he observed the different colors in the loop weave of the reception rooms carpeted floor. 

"I see", said the man behind the desk, folding his hands nervously. "What's your wife's name", he asked, his eyes narrow with concern.

John replied with  Mary's full name and after finding information in the computer the old man sent him to the elevator, the morgue was in the basement. He pressed the button marked 'B', the elevator moved. The elevator stopped, doors sliding open. The unpleasant smell of antiseptic washed over him. He approached another front desk.

"Can I help you?" asked the woman sitting behind the desk. She typed the information into the computer and said, "I'll be right back in a moment, could you wait here".  and she walked into the back of the morgue.

John stood there under the tinged green glow of the fluorescent lights. He reached out and touched a countertop, cold, stainless steel. He ran his hand, palm down, over the smooth surface. 

The woman walked back into the room. "John, there's been some kind of mix-up...", her voice cracking, she paused and coughed to clear her throat.

John felt cold and numb. It's happening again, he thought, it's getting worse, it keeps getting worse.

"Your wife...", she cleared her throat again, "your wife's body should be here. The computer says she's here, but we can't find her." She looked around as if searching for something.

John was confused, "Is she not dead?" It came out slow, his large, baggy, red eyes looked scared to hope.

The woman looked down, then raised her gaze to look John in the eyes, "I'm sorry John, she is deceased, and her body should be here. I have no idea what could have happened. We'll have to check other hospitals. Do you need someone to call? I have to make some phone calls about this. Do the police know how to reach you once this is sorted?"

John shook his head yes, looking away from her, confused and hurting. He turned to leave and felt a hand on his back. "I'm sorry", said the woman, trying to be helpful. John walked away, head lowered, barely noticing the warm gesture.

It was raining harder now, John switched on the windshield wipers. He thought about not going home, no one was there. Mary wouldn't be, Tabitha wouldn't be. He was alone with this incredible weight in his chest, this deep ache. But there wasn't anywhere else to go. 

He pulled into the driveway. The clouded, rainy, weather made the house look grey and diffuse. With no lights on in the house the windows were black like the empty, cold, river water. The hollow feeling house brought the same chilly dread.

                                                                                                 ***

John tried to make dinner alone that night. He cooked rice and beans on the stove-top. He set down a plate and got a wooden spoon to serve himself.  Pausing, he picked up that plate with both hands and brought it smashing down on the tiled counter. It shattered into hundreds of pieces. He held the shards that remained in his hands. As he loosened his shoulders and cried the shards fell  to the floor. All the little pieces, he looked around at the shards now speckled around him.

John got the broom and swept all the tiny shards into a pile. He noticed a small drop of blood on his hand. He noticed the diamond shape pattern of the broken ceramic shards as they slid off of the dustpan into the trash bin.

Getting a fresh plate he ate dinner, then did the dishes. Slowly, he shuffled off to bed, climbing the stairs towards his empty bedroom. 

He prepared for bed and lay down, hoping for sleep, fearing his thoughts and memories.  Face down he buried his head in his pillow and quickly drifted off to sleep.

                                                                                                 ***

John awoke with the feeling that he was being watched. "Mary?", he said aloud, as if talking to a dream or a ghost. Something made a noise in the darkness from across the room. John went still, he looked around, waiting for his eyes to adjust to the darkness. John was terrified, he assumed someone had broken into the house and was now in the room with him, staring at him from the darkness. A scared, "Hello", was all he could get out. 

Something, a small animal, ran from the room and down the stairs. 

John leapt from his bed. Putting his pants on he felt a cool breeze. Barefoot and shirtless he galloped down the stairs, jumping the last steps. The front door was wide open. The doors curtains billowed and waved out into the night. He pushed the door closed, The wind pulled it and slammed it shut. He hoped the animal from upstairs had run out the door. He walked into the kitchen to check the back door before going back to bed.

There in the middle of the dark kitchen stood Mary, her back was turned, she stood at the sink staring out the window. Layers of tension and grief melted away, he was engulfed with a warm feeling of relief. The broken heart that had spread into infinite, tiny fragments across the universe re-solidified. He was whole again. His eyes grew wide and starry, his strained expression turned to a smile. "I knew it",  he said. 

He stood there staring at her, she didn't say a word or move at all. John wasn't sure if he could move, he felt frozen. Had time stopped, am I dreaming. Nothing moved, nothing made a sound.

Then, he was sure he saw it this time, Tabitha ran past the kitchen door.
